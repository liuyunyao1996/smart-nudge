## 1. Taiwan proposes agentic AI governance framework for financial institutions

**Attention:** HIGH | CONSULTATION

Taiwan’s Financial Supervisory Commission opened a 30-day consultation on secure use of agentic AI by financial institutions, including insurers. The proposed framework would govern each use case according to its purpose, accountable parties, available data and tools, permitted actions, output destinations, and arrangements for monitoring, shutdown, recovery and auditability. It is consultative and would not replace existing laws, outsourcing controls or internal-control requirements.

- Risk classification would consider data sensitivity, the impact of permitted actions, and capability boundaries or propagation potential.
- Proposed controls incorporate zero-trust principles, including continuous verification, least-privilege access and traceability.
- The framework addresses the full operating lifecycle, including oversight, emergency shutdown, recovery and audit records.

**Potential impact:** If adopted, the framework could materially shape governance architecture and control costs for insurers deploying autonomous or semi-autonomous AI in Taiwan, while potentially influencing supervisory approaches elsewhere in Asia.

## 2. MAS sets enhanced safeguards for public prospecting

**Attention:** HIGH | GUIDANCE

The Monetary Authority of Singapore announced that financial institutions will be expected to restrict public prospecting to designated areas at commercial premises and provide qualified supervision of representatives. Representatives will also be expected to identify their marketing purpose at the outset and obtain consumer consent before continuing. MAS plans to issue updated marketing and distribution conduct guidelines in 2027, followed by a nine-month implementation period.

- Financial institutions will be expected to appoint suitably qualified individuals to supervise representatives conducting public prospecting events.
- Representatives must disclose that they intend to market financial products or services before seeking the consumer’s consent to continue.
- Street canvassing and door-to-door prospecting will remain prohibited.

**Potential impact:** The safeguards could alter insurer event-based acquisition processes, supervisory staffing and representative conduct controls in Singapore, with implications for distribution productivity and customer-consent documentation.

## 3. Mainland China finalises property insurance product-development rules

**Attention:** HIGH | FINAL RULE

The National Financial Regulatory Administration issued 41 articles across eight chapters governing property insurance product development, effective 1 November 2026. The provisions assign insurers legal responsibility for policy terms and rates, establish end-to-end governance from planning through regulatory filing, and require remediation or withdrawal reviews for products already on sale. They also introduce annual reporting and internal accountability requirements.

- The provisions identify eight categories of property insurance products that companies may not develop.
- Insurers must establish a product management committee led by the company’s principal officer.
- Requirements cover primary and supplementary coverage as well as internal product review and regulatory filing.
- Existing products must be assessed and, where necessary, remediated or withdrawn.

**Potential impact:** The rule materially raises governance and lifecycle-management expectations across Mainland China’s property insurance sector and could affect product approval timelines, portfolio rationalisation and management accountability.
