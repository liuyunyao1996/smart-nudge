## 2. NFRA outlines full-coverage supervision and insurance growth priorities

**Attention:** HIGH | INFORMATIONAL

At the 2026 Financial Street Forum on 19 October, NFRA Director Ding Xiangqun set out a risk-based supervisory direction centred on early warning, early correction, classified and look-through oversight, and full regulatory coverage of financial activities. She said high-risk institutions would continue to be handled through mergers, restructuring and market exit, while enforcement would address disorderly competition, non-compliant marketing and responsibility for major financial risks. The remarks were policy intentions rather than binding rules and also supported growth in pension, health, catastrophe, marine and technology insurance and greater participation by qualified foreign institutions.

- Supervisory attention will include cross-regional and cross-departmental activity, new businesses and products, third-party cooperation and cybersecurity.
- The NFRA intends to close regulatory gaps and apply full-chain oversight rather than relying only on entity-level supervision.
- Qualified foreign institutions were encouraged to participate in wealth-management, pension and health, and green-finance pilots.

**Potential impact:** The stated direction potentially increases conduct, governance, third-party and technology-risk scrutiny across Mainland China while creating opportunities for established foreign insurers in health, pensions and other policy-supported segments.

## 5. Taiwan FSC changes insurers’ internal-audit reporting

**Attention:** HIGH | GUIDANCE

Taiwan’s FSC directed insurers to submit prescribed internal-audit information through its single reporting portal, covering annual plans, execution results, control deficiencies, conclusions and audit personnel. Results must be filed within two months when planned audits identify systemic or materially consequential deficiencies or when an audit is conducted outside the annual plan. The FSC withdrew several earlier reporting letters with immediate effect and may require chief auditors or audit heads to report when audit performance or regulatory reporting is deficient.

- The required forms standardise reporting of audit planning, findings, conclusions and staffing information.
- Duplicate filing is waived when equivalent information has already been formally submitted to the FSC.
- The two-month filing requirement is triggered by specified significant findings and audits outside the approved annual plan.

**Potential impact:** The changes increase the consistency and timeliness of regulatory visibility into insurers’ internal controls and place greater accountability on senior audit leadership in Taiwan.
